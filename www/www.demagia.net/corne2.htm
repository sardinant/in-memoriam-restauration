The occult philosophy of Agrippa, published in 1533, is an extensive collection of magic combinations and correspondences. He reveals the exact keys for making talismans,
the magic inherent in numbers and words, and in the same vein as Hermes and the Picatrix, a list of planetary images.

In Agrippa's view, the universe is divided into three worlds: the elementary, the celestial and the intellectual. That is why his work scrupulously follows this breakdown of the cosmos. Book I talks about the material world, comprising the four elements, book II deals with the celestial world with the zodiac and the supernatural bodies, and book III focuses on the spiritual world of angels, intelligent life forms and ideas.
 

All worlds are interconnected according to a hierarchical model. The spiritual created the celestial, which in turn created the material. The link between the top world and the elementary one is established through astrology, hence the idea of sympathetic and astral magic. 

Agrippa's work is vital, because it specifically gives all the astral sympathies, the characters of the planets and their links with the animal, mineral and vegetable world.

The table of the Sun comprises a square with six columns and six lines. It contains 36 numbers, all of which add up to 666. Each line, column and diagonal adds up to 111.
If engraved on a strip of gold representing the fortunate Sun, this square will bring glory and power.

Similarly, Agrippa lists the Roman and Greek numbering systems. The Greek system is derived from the alphabet and divided into three classes: the first indicates the units, the second the tens and the third the hundreds.

 

A small comma beneath a letter means that it is multiplied by 1000.
   
Agrippa also studies the correspondences between the Chaldean esoteric signs (in the first column, the 12 signs of the zodiac can be recognised, followed by the seven planets, the four elements and the spirit of the world), the Hebraic letters (2nd column) and the Greek letters (4th column).

Agrippa also gives the exact use of each astral image:
 
•

Jupiter increases happiness, provides wealth and honour, benevolence and prosperity, and delivers man from the hands of his enemies.
•

Mars makes man powerful, and instils hardiness, courage and happiness.
•

Saturn increases life expectancy, fights against renal disease, increases various things and predicts the future.
•

The Sun makes man invincible, banishes vain delusions and helps to fight against fever and plagues.
•

Venus allows people to obtain favour and kindness, help men to gain the love of women,
creates beauty and makes man calm, pleasant, strong and alert.

•

Mercury gives science, eloquence, skill and money in business, cures fevers and instils genius and memory.
•

The Moon is a remedy for tiredness from travelling, multiplies and increases things rising from the ground and fights disabilities in children.
 

Finally, he reveals the signs of each planet to be inscribed on the talisman, according to the heavenly body that you wish to invoke.

Therefore, if you wish to make a talisman to increase your life expectancy, simply copy the sign of Saturn onto a sapphire, lodestone or brown jasper, and add black fig, mandrake or cypress extracts according to the exhaustive sympathies detailed by Agrippa.
