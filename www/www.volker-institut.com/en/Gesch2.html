The project for a German institute specialised in archaeology and palaeography came about towards the end of the 1950s, just as Peter Volker, an eminent research fellow at the University of Berlin, was starting his thesis on Greek epigraphy. Aware of the problem associated with widely-dispersed documentation, he decided to build a complete corpus of Greek inscriptions, which to this very day remains a benchmark in the world of research.

The idea of a foundation combining both a laboratory and a wealth of documentation within the same building officially took shape in 1965 under the name of the Institute of the Languages and Culture of Ancient Worlds. As part of its aim to bring research perspectives into line and allow for closer teamwork between archaeologists and palaeographers, it was built around an analysis laboratory and a significant range of equipment for treating manuscripts.

In 1976, the foundation was renamed the Volker Institute in homage to Professor Volker, who went missing the year before in Greece. It was reorganised into research units, and a comparative research centre for ancient cultures was created alongside the laboratory for epigraphy, codicology and papyrology. Today, the institute has three units under the responsibility of specialists in the field, who at the same time offer supervision for PhD students.
