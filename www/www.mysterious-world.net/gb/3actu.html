In her last opus, Patricia Cornwell goes back over the most legendary serial killer of Victorian England. Although armed without any irrefutable proof, the author doesn't pull any punches using a series of unsettling clues to point the finger of blame at Walter Richard Sickert, an English painter at the turn of the 20th century.

Cornwell's investigation is based more on psychology than police work and initially analyses the behaviour of both individuals before comparing the artist's paintings of prostitutes with photos taken of the victims at the morgue.

That is how the specialist in forensic novels claims to see Sickert's indisputably morbid works as a confession of his crimes. Convinced by the accuracy of her theory, Patricia Cornwell continues her investigation by analysing the painter's writing paper with that used for the letters allegedly sent by the ripper. The similarity in paper adds weight to her opinion, which is reinforced by the strange analogy between the pseudonym Nemo used by Jack to sign his letters, and which happened to be the stage name used by the painter when trying to break into acting!
