The wisdom of the Kabbalah picks up where philosophy leaves off.

Rabbi Nahman of Braslav

Universe of the Kabbalah

Kabbalists believe in the existence of an impersonal God, the En-Soph ("which is infinite"), who remains eternally unknowable in the depths of his being.

Knowledge of God

The Kabbalah endeavours to reconcile the En-Soph with the personal God of the Bible. This effort results in a whole range of attempts giving rise to the doctrine of the sefirot which, although expressed very differently by the masters of the Kabbalah, represents the essence of their teachings. According to some, the 10 Sefirot taken as a whole are none other than God, with each one presenting a face of the infinite; according to the others, they are merely the instruments of the divine power; God is immanent in them, but he remains beyond what is perceived in them. The sefirot are degrees of the being, the divine force, categories in the Aristotelian sense, numbers, spheres, vases that reflect the divine light in different colours, names of God, and finally degrees through which God descends from his retreat until his revelation in the Shekhinah, the creator's spark. It is difficult to define them. They have their source in the higher and infinite world, but are conditions of the "finite". They exercise their activity in the three lower worlds: creator ideas, creator forces, creator forms.

Human souls, which are primitively united in Adam, were separated after the fall and reunited in the body. They are formed by three elements that participate in the three worlds. After death, the first element returns to the light of God, the second experiences the beatitudes of paradise, and the third rests in peace on earth. That is how it is for the souls of the righteous; those of the sinners must achieve the perfection of their earthly existence through transmigration into other human bodies.
