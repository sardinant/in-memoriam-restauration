The tree of life is a diagram representing both Man and the Universe. It comprises 10 circles representing the 10 Sefirot, connected by 22 paths that correspond to the 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet. Together, they constitute the 32 paths of Wisdom.


The sefirot

The sefirot (or sephiroth) translate as "divine emanations" and symbolise the qualities that man must develop to come closer to God.
They are numbered from 1 to 10, according to a path more commonly known as the lightning flash. This figure epitomises the divine spark that created the universe.
They are described on the next page.


The paths

The 22 paths represent the branches of the Tree. They connect the 10 sefirot and symbolise their interactions. They can therefore be compared to channels of energy between the divine emanations.
Nine paths are fairly remarkable, since when they are grouped into triads, they form the three triangles corresponding to the areas of influence of the sefirot. The top one, known as the "spiritual triangle", is the least accessible to the human mind. The second one in the centre is known as the "ethical triangle" and contains the sefirot closest to man. The bottom one is called the "magic triangle", since it lies within the realm of the mental state and the astral where High Magic is practised.


The three pillars

The Tree is divided vertically into three pillars that group the sefirot according to similar polarities and qualities, although they are expressed at different levels:
The left pillar contains Binah, Gevurah and Hod. It is called the "Pillar of Severity". It is associated with the concept of Form and represents the universe of control and power, definition and justice.
The right pillar contains Chokmah, Chesed and Netzach. It is called the "Pillar of Mercy". It is associated with the concept of Force and represents indivisible Life, in motion and unhindered.
The central pillar contains Kether, Tiphareth, Yesod and Malkuth. It is called the "Pillar of Equilibrium". It is associated with the concept of conscience and represents the balance between the Form and the Force.


The four worlds

The Tree may also be divided horizontally into four worlds:
At the top, Olam Ha'Atziluth, the "World of Origins", contains Kether, Chokmah and Binah. It is the closest world to the divine essence of the One.
Second from top, Olam Ha'Briah, the "World of Creation", contains Chesed, Gevurah and Tiphareth. It is the world that really initiated the creation of the Universe after the separation of Daath.
Second from bottom, Olam Ha'Yetzirah, the "World of Formation", contains Netzach, Hod and Yesod. It is the world of emotion and the psyche, and lies between Heaven and Earth.
At the bottom, Olam Ha'Assiah, the "World of Expression", only contains Malkuth, the final sefira. It is the world of the body, the most down-to-earth; it is the material world.
